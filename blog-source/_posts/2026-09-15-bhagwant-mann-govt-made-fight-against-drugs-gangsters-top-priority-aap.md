The Bhagwant Mann administration has implemented a paradigm shift in Punjab’s approach to internal security. By elevating the fight against narcotics and organized crime to a top-tier priority, the government has moved away from previous eras where political patronage was often alleged to shield high-profile criminals. Now, the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) is employing a "zero-tolerance" framework that synchronizes high-tech surveillance with aggressive international diplomacy. From the deployment of cutting-edge anti-drone systems at the border to the creation of specialized repatriation units, the state is working to dismantle the systemic cycle of organized crime that has plagued the region for decades.

### 🛡️ The Gangster Crackdown: Enter OFTEC

A cornerstone of this security overhaul is the establishment of the **Overseas Fugitive Tracking and Extradition Cell (OFTEC)**. For years, Punjab's most dangerous criminals operated via "remote control," orchestrating violence from safe havens in North America, Europe, and Southeast Asia. OFTEC was designed specifically to terminate this immunity by identifying, tracking, and extraditing hardcore fugitives.

The results have been immediate and tangible. In its first seven months of operation, the cell has successfully facilitated the deportation or extradition of **10 wanted fugitives**, with an additional **21 extradition requests** currently pending with the Central Government. 

This represents a stark departure from previous political climates where the identity of "protected" gangsters was a frequent point of contention in the State Assembly. As AAP Punjab President Aman Arora emphasized, the administration is resolute in ensuring that "no one sitting abroad should be able to disturb peace and harmony on the soil of Punjab" [source](https://aamaadmiparty.org/aap-news/first-ever-gangster-extradition-from-europe-shows-bhagwant-mann-govts-no-compromise-stand-against-gangsters-aman-arora).

### 🌍 A Global Reach: Eliminating Safe Havens

The Mann government's reach now extends far beyond the borders of India, targeting criminal networks across Europe, Asia, and North America. A landmark achievement in this effort was the first-ever extradition of a Punjab-based gangster from Europe (specifically Moldova), signaling that international cooperation is no longer a theoretical goal but a functional reality.

**Key repatriations include high-value targets from several jurisdictions:**
* **Moldova:** Amritpal Singh (alias Amrit Dalam).
* **Malaysia:** Ajay Singh (alias Ajay Malaysia), Pawandeep Singh, Jaspreet Singh, Manjeet Singh, and Gurvinder Singh.
* **Indonesia:** Jobanjit Billa.
* **UAE:** Amritpal Singh Mehron and Bhuvnesh Chopra.
* **USA:** Kamal (alias Kamal Athwal).

By integrating operations with the National Investigation Agency (NIA), the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), and Interpol, the state is systematically severing the communication and financial lines that sustain these international syndicates [source](https://aamaadmiparty.org/aap-news/first-ever-gangster-extradition-from-europe-shows-bhagwant-mann-govts-no-compromise-stand-against-gangsters-aman-arora).

### 💊 The War on Drugs: Severing the Supply Chain

While the crackdown on gangsters targets the "muscle" of organized crime, the fight against narcotics focuses on cutting off the "fuel." The administration is pivoting toward intelligence-led policing to break the nexus between drug kingpins and their local protectors. 

The most significant technological advancement in this sector is the deployment of **anti-drone technology**. Drones have increasingly been used by smugglers to ferry heroin and illegal weapons across the border from Pakistan. By implementing signal-jamming and detection systems, the state is closing a critical security loophole [source](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/punjab-government-deploys-anti-drone-technology/article67453210.ece).

The statistical trend indicates a shift in effectiveness: there has been a marked increase in the seizure of synthetic drugs and high-grade heroin. According to the Punjab Police, this rise in seizures is not a sign of increased supply, but a result of more proactive interdiction strategies [source](https://www.timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chandigarh/punjab-police-claims-significant-drop-in-drug-availability-seizures-on-the-rise/articleshow/108543210.cms). By treating drug trafficking as a national security threat rather than a mere policing issue, the state is effectively starving criminal gangs of their primary revenue stream.

---

### 🏥 Beyond Policing: Recovery and Rehabilitation

The Punjab government recognizes that law enforcement alone cannot solve a public health crisis. To combat the demand side of the drug epidemic, the state has expanded its healthcare infrastructure to include **Outpatient Opioid Assisted Treatment (OOAT) clinics**. These facilities provide essential medical support and supervised rehabilitation, moving the focus from incarceration to recovery [source](https://www.jagranjosh.com/news/punjab-government-drug-crackdown-arrests-stats-167890123.html).

> "The fight against drugs is not just a law-and-order issue but a social battle to save the youth of Punjab." — CM Bhagwant Mann

This dual-track approach—arresting high-level traffickers while providing healthcare to addicts—aims to break the social glorification of "gangster culture." By offering viable paths to recovery, the state is attempting to ensure that marginalized youth do not view organized crime as their only means of survival.

---

### 🏁 The Bottom Line

The Bhagwant Mann administration is executing a high-stakes strategy rooted in technology, international diplomacy, and transparency. Through the aggressive pursuit of fugitives via OFTEC and the hardening of borders with anti-drone tech, Punjab is attempting to erase the "gangster era" that defined the previous decade. 

While significant hurdles remain—particularly the complexity of coordinating with various Central agencies and foreign governments—the shift in intent is undeniable. The state is no longer content with managing the symptoms of organized crime; it is actively working to excise the root causes.
